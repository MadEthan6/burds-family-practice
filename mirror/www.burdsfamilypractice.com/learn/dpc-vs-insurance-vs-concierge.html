Understanding Your Options

Insurance-Based, Direct Primary Care, or Concierge Medicine — Which One Is Actually Right for You?

The main difference between these three models comes down to who's in the room between you and your doctor — and how you pay. Traditional insurance-based care routes everything through a third party, creating prior authorizations, copays, and rushed appointments. Concierge medicine adds a premium membership on top of insurance. Direct primary care removes insurance from primary care entirely — you pay your provider directly, every month, for unlimited access. Burds Family Practice offers both insurance-based and DPC — and for most of our patients, DPC is the smarter, simpler choice.

What It Actually Costs

The Hidden Cost of "Free" Insurance-Based Care

Most people think of insurance-based primary care as low-cost because the copay is all they see at the time of the visit. But that copay is only one piece of what you're actually paying.

Every pay period, a health insurance premium is deducted from your paycheck — whether you've used your insurance that month or not. According to the 2025 KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey, the average employee contributes $571 per month out of their own paycheck toward a family health insurance premium. That's $6,850 a year — gone before you've seen a single doctor.

Then add the per-visit costs. Copays of $25 to $60 each time you come in. A deductible — often $1,500 to $6,000 or more for a family — that has to be met before your insurance pays much of anything. Surprise bills from coding errors or denied claims. The cost of not going in when you should have because you didn't want to trigger another charge.

When you add it all up — premium, copays, deductibles, and out-of-pocket expenses — traditional insurance-based primary care is far from free. And unlike your DPC membership, not a single dollar of that premium is predictable, transparent, or in your control.

Is DPC Right for You?

Who Does Best with Direct Primary Care

DPC isn't for everyone — but for many of our patients, it's a significantly better experience at a comparable or lower total cost.

DPC tends to be the right fit for…

Patients who value access, clarity, and relationship

  • Self-employed individuals and freelancers who pay their own healthcare costs
  • Small business owners and their employees
  • Families with children who need frequent sick visits
  • Patients managing one or more chronic conditions
  • Anyone on a high-deductible health plan tired of paying out of pocket for every visit
  • Patients who've felt rushed, overlooked, or underserved in the traditional system
  • Anyone who wants to text their provider directly instead of playing phone tag
  • Families who want predictable, budgetable healthcare costs
Insurance-based may still make sense if…

The traditional model fits your situation

  • Your employer covers most of your insurance premium and copays
  • You rarely need primary care and have low out-of-pocket costs currently
  • You have a complex medical history requiring frequent specialist coordination billed through insurance
  • You prefer not to pay a monthly fee for healthcare regardless of usage

Burds Family Practice accepts both insurance and DPC members. We'll never pressure you — but we're happy to walk through the numbers to help you decide what makes sense for your family.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is direct primary care (DPC)?
Direct primary care is a membership-based healthcare model where patients pay a flat monthly fee directly to their provider — with no insurance billing, no copays, and no surprise charges. The membership covers unlimited visits, same-day or next-day appointments, telehealth, chronic condition management, and direct access to your provider by phone or text. At Burds Family Practice in Peosta, Iowa, DPC membership starts at $99 per month for a single adult, $79 per month for a spouse, and $49 per month per child.
What is the difference between direct primary care and concierge medicine?
Direct primary care and concierge medicine are both membership-based models, but they differ significantly in cost and insurance involvement. DPC replaces insurance billing for primary care entirely — patients pay a monthly fee and the practice covers primary care with no copays or deductibles. Concierge medicine charges a premium annual membership fee — typically $2,000 to $10,000 per year — and still bills insurance on top of that fee. DPC is designed to be affordable and accessible to middle-income families. Concierge medicine is positioned as a premium, luxury service.
Do I still need health insurance if I join a DPC practice?
Yes. Direct primary care covers primary care services — your everyday healthcare needs, chronic condition management, preventive care, and sick visits. It does not replace insurance for hospitalizations, specialist referrals, imaging, surgery, or emergencies. Most DPC patients pair their membership with a lower-cost, higher-deductible health plan or a health sharing plan, since DPC handles the high-frequency, lower-cost visits that would otherwise eat through a deductible quickly.
How much does direct primary care cost at Burds Family Practice?
Burds Family Practice DPC membership is $99 per month for a single adult, $79 per month for a spouse or partner, and $49 per month per child. A family of four would pay $276 per month — with unlimited primary care visits, same-day appointments, telehealth access, and no copays or deductibles for covered primary care services.
Who is direct primary care right for?
Direct primary care is a strong fit for self-employed individuals and small business owners, families looking for predictable monthly costs, patients with high-deductible health plans who want to reduce out-of-pocket spending on primary care, anyone who wants same-day access and longer appointments, and patients who are frustrated with rushed visits and insurance complexity in the traditional model. Burds Family Practice in Peosta, Iowa offers DPC for patients of all ages.
Does Burds Family Practice accept insurance?
Yes. Burds Family Practice in Peosta, Iowa accepts both insurance-based patients and DPC members. We offer both models because we know different patients have different needs and situations. Our team is happy to walk through the comparison with you to help you decide which model makes sense for your family.
